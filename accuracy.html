Accuracy & Methodology

Last updated · Sep 2, 2026 · We audit this page every release cycle

CardResell exists so card sellers can pick the venue that actually pays them the most. That only works if you can trust the numbers. This page publishes exactly how we calculate them, where the source data comes from, and what we know we get wrong.

Live pricing

When you look up a card, we fetch its live market price from up to three sources in parallel and merge them into a single price ladder per printing (Normal, Holofoil, Reverse Holo, Unlimited, 1st Edition, etc.).

  • TCGplayer Market Price — pulled via the official TCGplayer / TCGcsv feed on every card load. This is the primary price for ungraded Pokémon, MTG, Yu-Gi-Oh!, Lorcana, and One Piece singles.
  • eBay sold comps — recent completed sales for the same card + printing, used to sanity-check TCGplayer and to power the sports-card path (eBay is the only real comp source for sports).
  • PriceCharting — used for graded values (PSA, BGS, CGC, SGC, TAG) and as a graded-tier backstop when TCGplayer's graded slot is empty. See Graded price sanity checks below.

High-price clamp. Net Payout is calculated from Market Value. "High" is display context only and is never the payout basis. If a feed's raw High comes back above 3× the current Market Price on the same card (or 3× Mid when Market is missing), we cap the displayed High at that anchor and keep the original as highRaw. This is why you'll sometimes see the raw High shown crossed out on a card — it's the correct behavior.

The clamp fires on every read path (fresh fetch, cached, printing-switch, grade-switch) so the same card can't show two different Highs depending on how you got to it.

Graded price sanity checks

Graded values (PSA 10, PSA 9, BGS 10, etc.) are harder than raw prices because different data sources use different sample sizes and different clean rules. Our current policy:

  1. PriceCharting wins over TCGplayer for graded slots. PriceCharting samples eBay sold comps directly and its numbers matched hand-audited reality more often (e.g. Mew GG10 PSA 10 was $557.50 on PriceCharting vs. $120 on TCGplayer's stale graded slot).
  2. 3-attempt retry with 12s timeout against PriceCharting, plus a 60s error cache so a bad response never spams the API.
  3. Grade-switch race protection — a _syncGen generation guard prevents stale grade-change results from overwriting a newer one when you rapid-flip between PSA 10 → PSA 9 → BGS 10.
  4. Reverse Holo / Holofoil dropdown stays pruned when you switch grades — no dead options bleeding across printings.

International & cross-border selling

Every payout figure on this site models a US seller shipping to a US buyer. The moment a border is involved the number changes, and on two of the fifteen venues you are not even contracting with an American company. Rather than bury that, each venue tile carries its own cross-border line — on locked rows too, so you know before you pay to unlock one.

Estimated grade scans

Our AI grading estimate is a screening tool, not a PSA replacement. It scores four sub-grades — Centering, Corners, Edges, Surface — and outputs an overall estimate on the PSA 1–10 scale. Two rules keep it honest:

  • The AI only sees what your camera shows it. A bad photo tanks the score. The photo-tips overlay walks you through capture rules before every grade scan.
  • We don't claim "within N points of PSA." We haven't published a validation set yet. The roadmap is a 100-card gold-set audit (paired PSA slabs + our estimate) — when that ships, the results land on this page with the raw comparison, not marketing copy.

Use grade scans to decide whether it's worth paying $25+ to submit a card. Don't use them to price a slab you don't own yet.

Card database coverage

The offline card index covers every Pokémon TCG set from Base Set (1999) through the latest release, refreshed weekly by an automated seeder. Non-Pokémon TCGs (MTG, Yu-Gi-Oh!, Lorcana, One Piece) are looked up live and don't require pre-seeding. Sports cards are keyed off eBay sold-comp search — accuracy scales with how specific the player + year + set query is.

Known upstream gaps:

  • 4 older McDonald's Pokémon promo sets have broken image URLs at the upstream image CDN. Prices work; images 404.
  • Alpha Black Lotus and a handful of reserved-list MTG cards have no TCGplayer market row — we fall back to eBay comps or return an honest "no comp data".
  • One Piece Luffy Leader mapping in the tcgcsv resolver is being tuned; one specific printing may resolve slowly.

Known limits

  • Buylist quotes are estimated, not live. Card Kingdom, CoolStuffInc, Star City Games, and TCG Bulk offer real quotes only after you build a cart on their site. Our ratios are honest averages; verify the exact number before shipping cards.
  • Cashout timing is not baked into net payout. COMC 2–6 wk intake, Fanatics vault dwell, Cardsphere 10% PayPal cashout — these are surfaced as red flags on each tile but they don't reduce the dollar number.
  • Sports-card sold comps depend on eBay availability. If eBay's Marketplace Insights API rate-limits us (403s from Vercel IPs happen), we fall back to sparser data. We flag this in the UI when it happens.
  • Cardmarket international shipping from a US seller is not modeled — you have to add that to your "Your shipping cost" field yourself.
  • eBay Top Rated seller discounts are user-input, not detected. If you toggle it on and you're not actually Top Rated, the number's wrong.

If you spot a fee that's out of date or a price that doesn't match a real sold listing, email us — we log every report and audit the flagged venue within a week.
