Pricing sources and limits

Prices depend on the card, printing, grade and available provider data. A fresh lookup can return a cached or older market reference. An estimate is not a buyer offer or proof of a recent sale of your card.

  • TCGplayer market references may arrive through TCG Price Lookup, game catalogues or TCGcsv. TCGcsv is a separate public data service; it is not an official TCGplayer-operated feed. Coverage and update times vary.
  • eBay sold comparisons are currently unavailable automatically in the app. Marketplace research links remain available so you can inspect comparable sales yourself and enter a price you have checked. An empty or unavailable feed is not evidence that no cards sold.
  • PriceCharting and SportsCardsPro supply reference values where a matching product and grade are available. Their guide prices are distinct from an itemized list of completed transactions.

High-price clamp. Net Payout is calculated from Market Value. "High" is display context only and is never the payout basis. If a feed's raw High comes back above 3× the current Market Price on the same card (or 3× Mid when Market is missing), we cap the displayed High at that anchor and retain the original for comparison. This is why you'll sometimes see the raw High shown crossed out on a card — it's the correct behavior.

The clamp fires on every read path (fresh fetch, cached, printing-switch, grade-switch) so the same card can't show two different Highs depending on how you got to it.

Highs and lows require reported endpoints. Price evidence uses TCGplayer listing lows and highs only for the selected condition and a provider update within seven days. These are asking prices, not a completed-sale range. When suitable endpoints are missing, CardResell shows one reference with an unavailable-range explanation; no percentage alternatives are calculated. A rolling sold average or one guide value does not establish a low and high.

Graded price sanity checks

Graded values (PSA 10, PSA 9, BGS 10, etc.) are harder than raw prices because different data sources use different sample sizes and different clean rules. Our current policy:

  1. PriceCharting wins over TCGplayer for graded slots. This is our source-priority rule, not a published accuracy benchmark or a guarantee that the reference matches your exact card.
  2. Unavailable values stay unavailable. A failed or missing price lookup is not a zero-dollar valuation.
  3. Selected grade stays consistent. A delayed price response for an earlier grade must not replace the grade you most recently chose.
  4. Reverse Holo / Holofoil dropdown stays pruned when you switch grades — no dead options bleeding across printings.

Three additional bulk selling options

The 18 supported selling options comprise 15 fee models above plus these 3 shipment workflows. Availability depends on your plan, card game, region and buyer requirements. Bulk offers are not inferred from retail card prices.

  • Full Grip Games: eligible English Pokémon bulk; enter a cash quote and shipping or preparation deductions.
  • Meta TCG: eligible English Pokémon bulk; check current acceptance and category rates, then enter the whole-shipment quote.
  • Card Conduit Standard: Magic shipments; the model deducts 10% of assessed buylist value plus $0.03 per processed card. Enter shipping and other deductions separately.

These bulk-program rules were checked October 13, 2026. They are not extra rows in the per-card fee ranking.

Sales tax and the fee base

A venue's fee rate is only half the number. The other half is what the rate is charged on. eBay includes buyer-paid sales tax in its fee base. By default, buyer tax is not estimated. You can now choose an optional eBay buyer-tax assumption in Advanced options. We apply that rate to the item price plus buyer-paid shipping, then include the estimated tax in eBay's final value fee, per-order threshold and any selected Promoted Listings fee. Tax is not seller income: only the fees charged on it reduce the payout. This scenario is shared by eBay comparisons, buy targets, grading estimates and draft previews during the current visit and resets on reload. Draft previews still exclude shipping. It is not a buyer-address lookup, a saved draft tax rate or an estimate of tax you owe; actual tax, shipping treatment and rounding can differ. Other venues retain their existing tax caveats. When tax is left unestimated, a tax-inclusive fee can run low by the fee charged on that tax.

What we will not do is let “we never checked” look like “we checked and it does not apply”. Those are different facts and they now have different values in the code. Of the fifteen ranked fee models: 2 are confirmed tax-inclusive, 5 are excluded, and 8 are unknown — the published fee page does not settle it. Unknown shows the caveat, so 10 of 15 venues carry the sales-tax row; before this pass, one did.

“Excluded” does not mean the buyer paid no tax. It means no fee we charge you against can contain it — because the venue publishes its fee as tax-exclusive, or because this model charges you no fee at that venue at all, so there is no base for tax to sit in. It is a statement about our arithmetic, not a tax determination about the venue, and nothing on this page tells you what tax a venue owes or collects. We got this wrong in an earlier draft of this table: four buylists were marked excluded on the reasoning that a store buying your cards has no buyer checkout, therefore no buyer tax. Whether a sale is taxable turns on the applicable rules and exemptions, not on the shape of the transaction, so that inference was not ours to make. One of the four (TCG Bulk) turned out not to be a direct buylist at all and is now unknown.

They say nothing about how much tax a buyer pays, who remits it, or what you owe — tax collected and remitted, the amount subject to fees, and your revenue are three separate quantities and we keep them separate. Verifying the tax base is also not the same as re-verifying a fee schedule, so the “Last verified” dates above did not move for this pass: we re-read all fifteen pages, but only for this question. Not one rate, cap or tier was re-checked.

International & cross-border selling

Every payout figure on this site models a US seller shipping to a US buyer. The moment a border is involved the number changes, and on two of the fifteen venues you are not even contracting with an American company. Rather than bury that, each venue tile carries its own cross-border line — on locked rows too, so you know before you pay to unlock one.

Estimated grade scans

Our AI grading estimate is a screening tool, not a PSA replacement. It scores four sub-grades — Centering, Corners, Edges, Surface — and outputs an overall estimate on the PSA 1–10 scale. Two rules keep it honest:

  • The AI only sees what your camera shows it. A bad photo tanks the score. The photo-tips overlay walks you through capture rules before every grade scan.
  • We don't claim "within N points of PSA." We haven't published a validation set yet. The roadmap is a 100-card gold-set audit (paired PSA slabs + our estimate) — when that ships, the results land on this page with the raw comparison, not marketing copy.

Use grade scans to decide whether it's worth paying $25+ to submit a card. Don't use them to price a slab you don't own yet.

Card database coverage

Catalogue coverage varies by game, set, language, and printing. A missing result does not mean a card does not exist. Confirm the set, number, variant, language, and condition before using a price. Available market prices are not a guarantee of recent sold transactions; eBay sold results may be unavailable inside the app. Use the marketplace search links to review comparable sales yourself, or enter a price you have verified.

Recovering a missed scan: single-card and binder text recovery can check names against the saved Pokémon English/Japanese, Yu-Gi-Oh!, Lorcana and One Piece catalogs. Magic uses Scryfall. Known upstream gaps:

  • Four older McDonald's Pokémon promo sets lack local reference images. Text identities remain searchable; live prices may be unavailable.
  • Alpha Black Lotus and a handful of reserved-list MTG cards have no TCGplayer market row — another reference may be available, or the card remains unpriced.
  • One Piece Luffy Leader mapping in the tcgcsv resolver is being tuned; one specific printing may resolve slowly.

Known limits

  • Buylist quotes are estimated, not live. Card Kingdom, CoolStuffInc, Star City Games, and TCG Bulk offer real quotes only after you build a cart on their site. Our ratios are modelling assumptions, not measured averages or current buyer quotes; verify the exact offer before shipping cards.
  • Waiting time is not a dollar deduction. Intake delays and payout timing matter even when estimated cash-out fees are already included. Read the fee breakdown and venue requirements.
  • Sports pricing may need manual research. Automatic eBay sold records are currently unavailable. Where a guide price is available it is labelled separately; otherwise inspect marketplace results and enter your own checked value.
  • Cardmarket account, shipping and currency assumptions. In the Venues picker, confirm that you have an eligible European account and enter international postage. Both are required before Cardmarket can rank; the entered postage is deducted from its payout. The model also deducts a 3% conversion allowance and approximates the €100 commission cap as $110. These are not live FX rates or proof that USD withdrawals are supported. Confirm your account currency, applicable conversion fees and actual shipping before relying on the estimate.
  • eBay Top Rated seller discounts are user-input, not detected. If you toggle it on and you're not actually Top Rated, the number's wrong.

If you spot a fee that's out of date or a price that doesn't match a real sold listing, email us — include the venue, source link and the difference you found so we can review it.
