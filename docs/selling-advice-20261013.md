# Value and preference based selling guidance

The owner clarified that people need to know what to do with inexpensive cards, before they have a buyer quote. The main card result now starts with “What should I do with this card?” A collection or bulk selection receives a starting plan before its fee tables, even if some values are missing. Buyer-quote forms in that planner are collapsed and optional.

Preferences: balance money/effort, most money, quickest sale or least work; willingness to ship; access to local selling. The single-card view also accepts a minimum proceeds amount worth packing a card (default $2), whether related cards are available to bundle, and explicit confirmation of entered shipping/packing costs. General preferences persist on this device. Card-specific confirmations reset when the result is recalculated.

The $5 boundary is explicitly a sorting guideline, not a market fact or a definition of bulk. Low-value cards can still warrant an individual listing when the user prioritizes money, confirms costs, and the current-fee estimate meets their own minimum. Otherwise the starting guidance is a related lot, setting the card aside for a future lot, or comparing a sorted buylist/dealer offer according to the preferences. Holding means waiting for a convenient sale, never a forecast of appreciation. Higher-value cards with weak net proceeds prompt a cost review rather than an automatic bulk recommendation.

The numerical reference is the highest estimated net from enabled, eligible listing venues with current fee schedules, independent of the selected display sort. Buylist ratios, stale fees and locked/disabled venues cannot drive that figure. It remains an estimate conditional on a sale, with tax-on-fee omissions and actual demand disclosed. No sales-volume signal, dealer quote, consignment outcome, lot discount or demand score is invented.

Graded cards are kept out of raw bulk. Missing/invalid prices require a value check; they are never classified as zero-value cards. Batch grouping uses per-card value and physical quantities and responds to selection/price changes. The guidance recommends checking wanted singles before category bulk and sorting remaining lots by game/set/character/team/playability. This is an initial rules-based decision aid, not a proven globally optimal sales plan.

Reference: TCGplayer's current bulk-listing instructions explicitly support collections, sets and bundles (re-read October 13 UTC): https://help.tcgplayer.com/hc/en-us/articles/360008108714-How-to-List-Bulk-Lots-on-TCGplayer
